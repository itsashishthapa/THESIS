Cookie factory constraint-violation documentation

The SAC policy produces continuous actions and is trained with soft penalties for
constraints that are not enforced directly. A violation is the positive residual
outside a constraint's feasible region. The comparison reports totals accumulated
over an evaluation window; therefore, an F4 value such as 24.021 is the sum of the
absolute F4 residuals over all time steps, not necessarily a 24.021-degree error at
one time step.

Constraints guaranteed by construction

1. Bypass constraint
   Requirement: beta1 + beta2 >= 1.
   Violation: max(1 - beta1 - beta2, 0).
   The signed beta command is mapped to discharge, idle, or charge such that the
   inactive storage branch always has beta=1. The SAC environment therefore cannot
   violate this constraint. The idle band is limited to [-0.05, 0.05] so exploration
   near zero is less likely to collapse into an always-idle storage policy. The
   constraint is omitted from the displayed violation breakdown.

2. Charge/discharge complementarity
   Requirement: charging and discharging must not occur simultaneously.
   The same beta-command mapping closes one branch exactly, making either Q_ch or
   Q_dch zero. Complementarity is consequently guaranteed for SAC and is omitted
   from the displayed breakdown.

3. F5 temperature coupling
   Requirement: T_I = T_3*beta2 + T_4*(1-beta2).
   T_I is calculated directly from this equation instead of being chosen as an
   independent action. Its SAC residual is therefore zero by construction, and F5
   is omitted from the displayed breakdown.

Constraints that can still be violated

1. F4 temperature coupling
   Requirement: T_2 = T_1*(1-beta1) + T_II*beta1.
   Violation: abs(T_2 - (T_1*(1-beta1) + T_II*beta1)).
   T_2 comes from the steam-generator surrogate, while T_1 and T_II depend on the
   storage state, HTHP surrogate, mass flow, and compressor speed. The beta command
   does not algebraically solve F4. The environment derives compressor speed by
   solving F4 within [0.8, 1.53], selecting the root nearest the previous R. If no
   feasible root exists, it applies the bounded value with the smallest residual and
   reports that residual to the next observation and the info dictionary.

2. Flow bounds
   Requirement: 0 <= Q_ch,Q_dch <= Q_flow_max, with Q_flow_max=5000.
   Negative or excessive flow can result from surrogate temperature differences and
   the selected mass flow. The action bounds alone do not guarantee these heat-flow
   bounds.

3. Grid-power bounds
   Requirement: 0 <= P_grid <= P_HP_max, with P_grid=P_HP-P_WT.
   A violation can occur when available wind exceeds HTHP demand, producing negative
   grid import, or when the HTHP surrogate produces power above its upper bound.

4. HTHP inlet-temperature bounds
   Requirement: 177 <= T_I <= 250 degrees Celsius.
   Although F5 is satisfied exactly, its derived T_I value can still fall outside
   the permitted interval because it depends on storage and process temperatures.

5. Storage-state bounds
   Requirement: T_s_min <= T_s_next <= T_s_max, currently 183 to 324 degrees Celsius.
   Charging or discharging changes T_s through the explicit storage-energy update.
   Large or prolonged flows can move the next state outside these bounds.

6. Terminal cyclic condition
   Requirement: T_s at the final step must equal the initial T_s_0.
   Intermediate actions can leave net stored or discharged energy at the end of the
   episode. This condition is penalized only at termination; exact return to the
   initial temperature is not guaranteed.

Reward penalties used for training

- lambda_bound=20.0 for storage-state violations.
- lambda_terminal=3000.0 for the final cyclic-temperature gap.
- lambda_coupling=500.0 for the F4 residual (F5 is exact).
- lambda_grid=3000.0 for grid-power violations.
- lambda_T_I=10.0 for inlet-temperature violations.
- lambda_flow=5000.0 for charging/discharging flow-bound violations.

The per-step penalties are normalized before being subtracted from the reward:
state and F4 by the storage-temperature range, T_I by its allowed range, flow by
Q_flow_max, and grid power by P_HP_max. The terminal penalty is applied only on the
last step and is normalized by the storage-temperature range. Lowering penalties
for constraints that were already satisfied makes F4 and the cyclic terminal target
relatively more important without increasing their own coefficients.

Interpretation of Pyomo residuals

Very small nonzero Pyomo values (for example, approximately 1e-8 to 1e-6) are
numerical solver residuals caused by finite feasibility tolerances. They do not
normally indicate a meaningful physical violation. In contrast, the larger SAC F4
and terminal values reflect genuine soft-constraint errors that training should
reduce. After retraining, the comparison should focus on F4, flow, grid power, inlet
temperature, storage state, and terminal cyclic violations.
